design philosophy

I'd follow one rule:

Every run should create a story worth telling.

Not a scripted story.

An emergent story.

Players love saying:

"You'll never believe what just happened..."

That's free marketing.

My game concept
Working title: "Pulse"

Genre

Endless Runner × Roguelite × Rhythm × Physics

Imagine:

Pepsiman
Mirror's Edge
TrackMania
Geometry Dash

combined into something original.

The Story

There is almost none.

You are a courier.

The city is alive.

The city is trying to stop you.

Deliver the package.

That's it.

Five seconds of explanation.

Gameplay starts immediately.

The Twist

The city is procedural.

But not only procedural...

It reacts to YOU.

Suppose you always jump.

The game notices.

Next streets:

More overhead obstacles.

Suppose you slide frequently.

Now:

Road barriers appear.

Suppose you love shortcuts.

The city starts trapping shortcuts.

The player feels:

"The city is learning."

Actually...

It's just changing weighted probabilities.

Almost no extra code.

Huge psychological effect.

The Secret Mechanic

Near misses.

Suppose this happens.

Truck.

Player.

Gap.

Only 10 cm.

The game notices.

Instead of:

+10 points

It gives:

"INSANE DODGE"

Combo ×6

Slow motion

Screen shake

Particles

Heartbeat sound

People LOVE that.

But here's the real trick...

Risk creates score.

Playing safely...

Always works.

Playing dangerously...

Creates ridiculous scores.

Now players start inventing crazy strategies.

Exactly what TrackMania players do.

Procedural Difficulty

Not:

Speed +1

Instead:

New combinations.

Examples:

Rain.

Fog.

Traffic jam.

Construction.

Broken bridge.

Power outage.

Police chase.

Fire.

Wind.

Nothing new to model.

Just combine systems.

Upgrades

Every minute:

Choose one.

Examples:

Double jump

Wall run

Long slide

Magnetic pickups

Dash

Air control

Perfect dodge bonus

Every run becomes different.

The Hook

Around minute three.

A ghost appears.

It isn't AI.

It is...

YOU.

Your previous run.

Now...

You race yourself.

Eventually:

Five ghosts.

From your five best runs.

The road fills with your own history.

That's memorable.

Procedural Music

No MP3.

Music generated from:

BPM
Chords
Bassline
Melody seed

As speed increases...

Music intensifies.

Players don't consciously notice.

But they feel it.

Visual Style

No realism.

I'd go for:

Flat colours.

Soft gradients.

Bloom.

Long shadows.

Stylised fog.

Simple geometry.

Very clean UI.

Think:

Low-poly meets synthwave.

Why?

Because shaders are cheap.

Textures are expensive.

The Psychological Hooks

Every 15 seconds:

Reward.

Every 30 seconds:

Decision.

Every minute:

Upgrade.

Every two minutes:

New event.

Every run:

Different city.

The "Impossible Escape"

Sometimes...

Very rarely...

The procedural generator creates:

An insane sequence.

Players barely survive.

The game celebrates it.

They immediately record the replay.

Then I'd add one mechanic that I think would become the game's signature
Flow Mode

The game quietly tracks:

Perfect jumps.

Perfect slides.

Perfect dodges.

No UI.

No tutorial.

No mention.

If the player strings together enough perfect actions...

The entire city transforms.

Colours shift.

Music evolves.

Particles explode.

Camera subtly widens.

Time feels faster.

Everything becomes beautiful.

Players discover this on their own.

Suddenly they aren't just trying to survive—they're chasing that feeling again.

Why I think this could win

Technically, it fits the constraints:

Procedural geometry instead of large models.
Shader-based materials instead of texture packs.
Procedural audio.
Compact executable.

Design-wise, it aims to satisfy the strongest replay drivers:

Fair challenge.
Constant adaptation.
Risk versus reward.
Mastery.
Discovery.
Personal competition.

But if I had to choose one thing that I believe separates memorable games from merely good ones, it would be this:

Give players a moment they didn't know was possible.

Not through a scripted cutscene or a huge boss fight, but through gameplay.

Imagine a player narrowly threading between two trucks, bouncing off a wall, triggering Flow Mode, overtaking their own ghost, and setting a new personal best—all in the span of ten seconds. That's the kind of sequence they tell their friends about.

And in a competition where every entrant has only 1.44 MB, the game that creates the best stories in the player's mind is often the one people remember long after they've forgotten how many polygons it used.
